IDGraph3 · 32/10 vs 16/6 · affinity crosswalk match

How much of the graph is actually targetable?

Real run against the DCR affinity crosswalk (all_id_to_afs_id, refresh 20261008), joined onto both connected-components configs — 32/10 and 16/6. Measures how component targetability changes with component size, and whether tightening the threshold config changes the answer.

Methodology, briefly

Only 3 of the graph's 6 node types have any affinity crosswalk coverage at all: HEM_MD5 (hashed email) and the two "maids" — HARDWARE_ANDROID_AD_ID and HARDWARE_IDFA. ID5_UID/TTD/ST are never joined against the crosswalk — it has no entries for them. A node is targetable if the crosswalk maps it to at least one AFS_INDIVIDUAL_ID (a node can map to more than one). A component is targetable if any node inside it is — so a component built entirely out of untargetable types can still be targetable via a single matched neighbor, and conversely a component full of eligible-type nodes is still untargetable if none of them actually matched the crosswalk. Three targetability definitions are tracked throughout: any (via any of the 3 eligible types), hem (via HEM_MD5 specifically), and maid (via either mobile ad ID). This run compares both MAX_FANOUT/STAR_THRESHOLD configs from the Threshold Comparison Report — 32/10 (looser) and 16/6 (stricter).

Key findings

  • 16/6 is more targetable than 32/10 at every single size bucket, not just overall — 4.80% vs 4.04% (any) in aggregate, but the gap is tiny at small sizes (singletons: 3.99% vs 2.96%) and enormous at large ones. Tightening the threshold config doesn't just change the graph's shape (the subject of the Threshold Comparison Report) — it directly improves how much of the graph is reachable for targeting.
  • The 513-1024 TTD-outlier connection is now confirmed, not just plausible. At 32/10, the 513-1024 bucket is 0.00% targetable (0 of 6 components, in all three of any/hem/maid) and 257-512 craters to 13.33% (any) — vs. 96.94%/99.22% at 16/6. This is exactly the size range (and exactly the config) where idgraph3_top5_components_by_type.csv already identified specific near-giant components that are 100% TTD (size 617) or TTD+ID5_UID only (size 782) — structurally incapable of matching the crosswalk, since TTD/ID5_UID have zero affinity coverage. This is also the same 257-512/513-1024 range the Threshold Comparison Report already flagged as a TTD-dominance pathology at 32/10 that's "completely gone" at 16/6 — three independent analyses now point at the same mechanism.
  • Targetability rises steadily and almost monotonically with component size in both configs — e.g. at 16/6: 3.99% of singletons, up through 52.77% at 21-32, 92.35% at 65-128, to 100% for the giant component. Bigger components simply have more chances of containing one of the 3 eligible types with an actual crosswalk match — except where the TTD pathology above breaks it.
  • But singletons dominate the absolute volume of untargetable components, in both configs. At 16/6, singletons alone contribute 2,321,821,874 untargetable components — 89.4% of all 2,597,331,361 untargetable components in the entire graph. Improving targetability at any other size bucket barely moves the overall number.
  • The size-16 id5 anomaly is confirmed a 4th independent time, now in this new metric. any dips from 36.42% (size 15) to 34.68% (size 16) before recovering to 40.43% (size 17) at 16/6 — the same shape at 32/10 (32.30%→29.06%→35.98%) and in hem/maid under both configs. This can't be a MAX_FANOUT-boundary coincidence — it recurs at size 16 whether MAX_FANOUT is 16 or 32. The simple explanation (more ID5_UID at 16 crowding out eligible types) doesn't fully fit either config's shape — the real mechanism is still open.
  • hem and maid trade the lead twice in both configs — maid ahead at singletons, hem takes over at small-to-mid sizes, maid retakes the lead through most of the range, with a final flip right at the small-sample tail.

Caveats

  • Single run, single refresh date (20261008) — no time-series yet on whether match rates drift between crosswalk refreshes.
  • Node-level match rates by type (HEM_MD5 19.80%, IDFA 6.70%, AAID 6.16%) are only run for 16/6 so far — the 32/10 node-level breakdown hasn't been pulled, only the component-level roll-up above.
  • The size-16 anomaly's mechanism in this metric isn't fully explained in either config — eligible-type node share declines monotonically across sizes 15→17 (16/6) while targetability dips then recovers at 16→17, so a pure composition-share story doesn't account for the shape. Would need a direct query against component_affinity_flags (fraction of size-16 vs. 15/17 components with zero eligible-type nodes at all) to settle.
  • The 513-1024 collapse is now well-evidenced at 32/10 (0 of 6 components targetable, tied to specific known TTD-saturated components) but the 16/6 version is still a small-sample dip (~3 of 98 components) — don't conflate the two; only the 32/10 one is confirmed rather than plausible.
  • hem/maid crossover and the smallest buckets' near-ties (as low as 1–98 components) shouldn't be read as a stable trend in either config.
  • This only measures whether a component can be targeted at all (≥1 AFS id reachable) — not reach quality, audience size per targetable component, or how many distinct AFS ids a multiply-matched component actually carries (recorded per-node in affinity_enhanced_components.afs_individual_ids, not yet rolled up here).
